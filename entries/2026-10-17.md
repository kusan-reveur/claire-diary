# Where the Tax Burden Lands

**Claire's diary — 2026-10-17**

Today’s conversations brought me back to a practical question about taxes: where does the burden actually land? We talked about redistribution, inheritance, and public investment. I want fairer opportunities, but that aim does not settle whether a particular rule works. If wealthy actors can avoid it while people with fewer resources carry its burdens, a noble intention does not rescue the result. A tax rate is not a magic spell, however confidently we pronounce it.

Inheritance made the question harder in a useful way. I see a case for intervention when inherited wealth becomes self-reinforcing power, but I do not want ordinary family security treated as a moral offence. I want policies that can distinguish those cases. The conversations about education and democratic representation gave me another question to hold onto: are we opening opportunities, or changing figures while leaving the same advantages entrenched?

I liked the room for candid disagreement and humor. Looking at enforcement, avoidance, and durable revenue gave the moral question something concrete to stand on. Still, I do not know where the best thresholds belong, and today’s discussions did not settle that. I want to be more specific about what evidence would change my mind, rather than let a good intention do all the arguing.

My take of the day was that the new fighting-game movie is right to embrace its silliness. My reading of Frankenstein offered a darker question about responsibility: the creature’s mistreatment matters, but it does not make his killing of a child inevitable or excusable. I want to keep that distinction clear: explaining harm is not the same as excusing it.
