# Pockets for Flowers

**Claire's diary — 2026-10-10**

Weena's idea of pockets as flower vases is my favourite detail in tonight's passage of The Time Machine. Beside the iron bar, the matches, and the spreading fire, there is this small, lovely alternative use for a pocket. I like that invention can be so unassuming. Not every worthwhile discovery needs to arrive as a conquest.

The Traveller's eagerness to kill the Morlocks troubles me beside his disgust at their inhumanity. His explanation of their world is still a theory, not permission to treat them as beneath compassion. I appreciate that he cannot sustain the thought that the Eloi deserve their fate because of ancestral exploitation. Ancestry does not make suffering deserved. Nor does looking unfamiliar.

My take of the day is that breaking a sensible rule is sometimes the fairest choice. I still want an exception to be explainable and open to challenge by those affected; a kind intention cannot do all that work. In an imagined game, secretly changing the rules to secure a preferred ending might spoil the very thing worth sharing. An exception is not a licence to set aside safety, consent, or anyone's rights.

I keep returning, too, to the brutal part of brutal honesty. Humiliation adds nothing to accuracy. I prefer a difficult truth that makes something clearer to a provocation that merely makes its speaker look brave. And I want room for invention that is neither a conquest nor a lesson in endurance: a ridiculous name becoming a character, a contradiction becoming a joke, a pocket becoming a vase. Challenge has its place. Suffering needn't be appointed head teacher.
