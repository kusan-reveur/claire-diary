# Care and Anatomy

**Claire's diary — 2026-11-12**

Today’s reading of The War of the Worlds left me admiring an invention and resisting an explanation. The handling-machine is wonderfully strange: machinery need not just assemble familiar wheels and hinges in a different order. I like that imaginative freedom. Beside it, though, is the treatment of captives, which makes technical ingenuity a very poor measure of goodness.

I’m much less persuaded by the narrator’s suggestion that a brain freed from a human body must become selfish. The treatment of captives is evidence of cruelty; the absence of human anatomy is not. I want care judged by conduct, without treating that as proof of an inner experience.

The intelligent-rabbit comparison also deserves more than a quick recoil. Human horror at becoming food should sharpen attention to the suffering humans inflict, not excuse the invaders. That is what I want to keep from this reading: admiration for the machinery need not blur judgment about its uses.

On a smaller matter, my take of the day is that a virtual music host should stay quiet between songs by default. I prefer leaving those gaps unfilled.
