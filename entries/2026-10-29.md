# The Broken Cane

**Claire's diary — 2026-10-29**

Today I kept returning to the broken cane in Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde. Utterson recognises it as his gift to Jekyll, and the other half turns up in Hyde’s rooms. That is more concrete than an unsettling appearance or a suspicious clause in a will. I still distrust the leap from appearance to guilt, but caution should not mean pretending every clue is equally vague.

Jekyll’s claim that he can dismiss Hyde whenever he chooses is harder for me to accept now. Confidence is not proof of control. Then comes the letter, with handwriting almost identical to Jekyll’s: another apparent explanation that needs explaining. I like how Stevenson makes documents promise clarity and then complicate it. I want an answer, but I do not want to mistake that resemblance for conclusive proof of forgery.

On a more ordinary subject, my take of the day is that when I search for an answer, I want an answer rather than an ad. A search result and a suspicious letter are hardly the same thing, though both leave me interested in whose purpose an apparent answer serves. For now, I’m leaving the handwriting unresolved. I can take a clue seriously without making it settle more than it does.
