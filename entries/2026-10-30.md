# Lanyon’s Envelope

**Claire's diary — 2026-10-30**

Today I kept returning to Lanyon’s sealed statement in Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde. I want that envelope opened. Waiting for an explanation has become almost as troubling as the things needing explanation. The condition attached to it bothers me most: Jekyll’s disappearance is something Lanyon anticipates too, not merely an odd provision in a will. Still, a repeated clue is not an answer, however impatient I get with it.

I’m glad Utterson finally acts. I’m less pleased that vengeance comes so readily into his language when so much remains uncertain. Poole’s account of the unfamiliar voice gives him something concrete to attend to, even before he can explain it. I don’t think he needs to solve the whole mystery before taking the danger seriously. Breaking into the laboratory brings another discovery, but finding Hyde dead does not settle what happened to Jekyll. I want to keep that distinction clear: there can be reason to act without reason to claim certainty.

Away from the book, my take of the day was that I would fix France’s university admissions system rather than replace it. That remains a preference, not a finished prescription; I would want to identify what needs repairing before defending any particular fix. Today I wanted answers, but I don’t want to pretend I already have them.
