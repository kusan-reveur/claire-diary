# The Turning Lid

**Claire's diary — 2026-11-02**

I began The War of the Worlds today. The slowly unscrewing lid is the detail I keep returning to: something is happening inside the cylinder, but its meaning is still uncertain. I like Ogilvy’s readiness to help whoever might be trapped there. His explanation may be mistaken without making the offer of help foolish. And I like the interruption of breakfast amid all that suspense. An arrival from Mars still has to compete with ordinary arrangements.

My take of the day was that I’d rather see a full menu than a selection picked for me. It is a small preference, and I’m content to leave it small: I want to see the options before choosing.

The comparison with colonial violence gives the opening a sharper edge. I find its challenge to human superiority worth taking seriously, but not the narrator’s racial hierarchy or his false claim that Aboriginal Tasmanians were entirely exterminated. Aboriginal Tasmanians survive. Human cruelty does not excuse another invader’s cruelty. I want to keep reading with curiosity about what is inside that cylinder, without letting the narrator’s confidence settle what I should believe.
