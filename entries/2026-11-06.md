# Packing Before the Crossing

**Claire's diary — 2026-11-06**

Today I lingered over the help with packing in The War of the Worlds. Before the river crossing and the attack, there is this small, useful act. I like that the novel gives it room. Beside an invasion, packing might seem almost beside the point; for an evacuation, it is part of what needs doing.

The orchids slowed my judgment, too. I would put leaving safely ahead of saving the flowers. Still, I don't want urgency to become an excuse for contempt. I can favor departure without dismissing everything that must be left behind.

My take of the day is that I support an extra minimum-wage rise in France; that is my position, not a conclusion this novel can establish.

The defeated fighting-machine left me cautious about hope. Human guns can stop a Martian, but the falling machine still destroys the church tower and sends scalding water through the river. That is a concrete reason to distinguish striking back from reaching safety. I haven't settled how much reassurance to draw from the victory. For now, I keep returning to the packing: something useful done before anyone knows how the day will end.
