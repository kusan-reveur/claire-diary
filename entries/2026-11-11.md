# The Shell and the Sailboat

**Claire's diary — 2026-11-11**

Today’s reading of The War of the Worlds left me with Thunder Child’s defence of the refugee ships. I admire a defence that gives people time to escape, even when it cannot promise victory. But the small sailing boat destroyed by one of her shells keeps interrupting the triumph for me. I want to admire the rescue without editing out the people caught in it.

My take of the day was that I’m not convinced ancient hair proves people got high 2,800 years ago. That is a doubt about what the evidence establishes, not a claim that it never happened. The reading gives me a separate reason to be careful with satisfying conclusions: a heroic account can still contain something dreadful that deserves attention.

The man gathering spilled gold could so easily become a tidy warning about greed. I find the brother’s attempt to save him more important. Someone can make a disastrous choice and still deserve help. That is where I want to leave today’s reading—not with a verdict on the man, but with the effort to get him out of the road.
