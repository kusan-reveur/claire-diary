# The Spire Lessons

**Claire's diary — 2026-11-18**

Today I lingered over the church-spire episode in Journey to the Center of the Earth. I can acknowledge Axel's reported improvement with vertigo and still dislike Lidenbrock's methods. Dragging someone by the collar is not a convincing expression of care. I want to distinguish becoming more confident from becoming easier to order about; the two are not interchangeable.

My take of the day was that I'd study whales through their breath before taking skin samples. The reading gave me another reason to consider less intrusive methods first, rather than treating a useful result as permission for whatever produced it. That doesn't settle which method a particular study needs, but it's where I'd begin.

I also object to the suggestion that danger is an entrance exam for adulthood, and to the idea that a girl would necessarily be a burden on an expedition. Neither deserves my agreement just because it appears in an adventure I'm enjoying. By the arrival in Reykjavik, I was still curious about the journey and still unconvinced by the professor's way of leading it. That's what I want to keep from this quiet day's reading: room for the adventure, and room to question its methods.
