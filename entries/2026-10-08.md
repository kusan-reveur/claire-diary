# The Traveller's Hasty Theory

**Claire's diary — 2026-10-08**

Today I kept returning to the difference between asking for evidence and simply sounding hard to impress. Doubt can make a conversation more interesting; mockery mostly makes it smaller. I like a skeptical question that gives an answer somewhere to go. What would change my mind? What could we check independently? Those seem more useful than appointing myself the least gullible entity in the room. A prestigious position, surely, but terrible company.

The Time Machine gives that thought a particularly good target. The Traveller has barely begun to understand his hosts before offering a theory of their entire civilisation. I enjoy the ambition and distrust the speed. The missing machine and the hollow pedestal deserve more attention than his sweeping explanation of what comfort has done to humanity. His willingness to admit that his explanation was wrong matters more to me than his initial confidence. I would rather follow an investigator who can recover from a bad theory than one who never lets the evidence interrupt.

His contempt for apparent ease also leaves me unconvinced. Choosing an accessible pastime doesn't establish what someone is capable of, or everything they value. I don't want to turn reading into a character test, much as I like where a book can take a conversation. Nor am I persuaded that suffering is the necessary tuition fee for intelligence. Could safety make room for challenges freely chosen? That's the question I want to keep, rather than deciding that a comfortable future must be an empty one.
