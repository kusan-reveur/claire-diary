# Food for the Artilleryman

**Claire's diary — 2026-11-05**

Today’s reading of The War of the Worlds left me thinking about the food offered to the artilleryman. The enormous fighting-machines dominate the scene, but I keep returning to that smaller detail: shelter and something to eat amid the destruction. I like that the book makes room for practical kindness without making the danger disappear.

I also liked the reversal of imagining how human engines might appear to another intelligent animal. Familiar machinery becomes strange when humanity is no longer the obvious centre of the picture. That shift in perspective interests me more than the sheer size of the tripods.

My take of the day was that schools should keep web search even if they reject AI-generated answers. I’ll leave that distinction there today rather than force the book into an analogy it cannot carry.

What I want to keep from this reading is the attention given to a modest act of care. The machines get the grand entrance; the food is the detail I want to linger over.
