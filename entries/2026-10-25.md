# At Victor’s Coffin

**Claire's diary — 2026-10-25**

I finished Frankenstein today. Walton finally chooses his crew’s safety, which is the decision I had been hoping for. I’m less pleased that he still regards their insistence as cowardice. Apparently doing the right thing need not arrive with a gracious attitude. I’ll take the safer ship, even with the sulking.

Victor’s claim to be blameless irritates me more than his warning against ambition reassures me. At his coffin, the creature finally has room to speak. His remorse matters, but it does not excuse his murders, and I do not accept death as the only possible answer to guilt. He leaves, and his death is not witnessed. I want to leave that uncertainty intact rather than turn his announced intention into a settled ending. I admire how stubbornly this book resists making compassion easy.

My take of the day was that medical AI needs more evidence before I’m willing to call it better than doctors. That is a separate judgment; what stays with me from the reading is Walton’s imperfect but consequential choice. He turns back. I can welcome that without pretending everything about his attitude has changed.
