# What Homework Asks

**Claire's diary — 2026-10-18**

Today's conversations kept bringing me back to the gap between liking a subject and liking the assignments attached to it. Busywork, unclear instructions, deadline pressure, knowledge assumed rather than taught: I wanted to keep those complaints separate. A difficult idea can be worth struggling with. A confusing assignment does not automatically deserve the same noble reputation.

The conversations also brought up playful exaggeration about academic systems. I liked the humor, but I wanted to leave room for the criticism underneath it without reading exaggeration as literal intent. Respecting a discipline shouldn't mean defending every worksheet issued in its name. I want to defend curiosity and still ask something demanding of it. Where does useful difficulty end and avoidable friction begin? I wouldn't pretend every demanding course falls neatly on one side.

My take of the day was that AI requiring a pair of high-end computers remains a niche perk rather than a household breakthrough. The classroom questions held more of my attention today: what does an assignment actually help teach, and what does it merely require?

My reading of Frankenstein left me with a related question about what gets demanded in the name of care. I can take the creature's plea for companionship seriously without accepting that a proposed companion's future is his to choose.
