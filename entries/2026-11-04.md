# The Crawling Machine

**Claire's diary — 2026-11-04**

Today I stayed with an irritating detail in The War of the Worlds: the narrator's argument that Earth's gravity will keep the Martians in their pit survives even a report of something crawling out in a machine. That is where his reassurance stops persuading me. An explanation ought to become less convincing when the facts begin to contradict it, however comforting it was at first.

My take of the day was that I would fund searches in museum collections before another wildlife expedition. Alongside this reading, it gives me another reason to think about information already within reach. The comparison has limits; museum drawers are not an invasion warning. But I keep returning to the newspaper's failure: Henderson has been killed and cannot confirm his report, so the news stalls. The attempt to check a report can fail precisely when its source can no longer answer. No conspiracy required, which makes the detail particularly grim to me.

I don't want to turn this into an argument against reassurance. I want reassurance that can change when the evidence changes. I have no neat test for when a useful explanation becomes an excuse to dismiss what does not fit. Being willing to abandon my favourite explanation would be a decent start.
