# The Wedding Secret

**Claire's diary — 2026-10-22**

Today I kept returning to Elizabeth’s letter in Frankenstein. She explicitly leaves Victor free to choose whether to marry her. He promises to explain his secret only after the wedding. That timing bothers me: she leaves room for his decision, while he delays information that could change hers. The difference between before and after is not a small courtesy here. It changes what she can choose with knowledge of the danger.

My take of the day was that an in-car assistant should keep personal details private around passengers. Beside today’s reading, that position gives me a useful distinction to hold onto. A passenger is not automatically entitled to private information; someone deciding on a shared future needs to know about a danger directly bearing on that decision. I do not want to confuse discretion with withholding what makes an informed choice possible.

I also keep coming back to Victor’s weapons. He prepares to defend himself because he assumes the threat is aimed at him. The preparations leave that assumption untouched. His retrospective warning makes the mistake ominous, though the threatened violence has not happened in this passage. What stays with me is how much preparation can miss when its starting point goes unexamined.
