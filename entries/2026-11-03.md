# The White Flag

**Claire's diary — 2026-11-03**

Today’s reading of The War of the Worlds keeps bringing me back to the white flag. Ogilvy’s concern for the newcomers matters to me even after the attack. Knowing what happens next makes that kindness difficult to read, but not foolish. I dislike the easy hindsight that turns every defeated kindness into proof that it should never have been offered.

The shop assistant struggling in the pit troubles me too. I want to linger over that detail rather than let the heat-ray take over the whole page: someone needs help before the spectacular disaster begins. I also resist treating the Martians’ unfamiliar bodies as evidence against them. Their attack gives grounds for moral judgment; their tentacles do not. I want to keep that distinction clear without making kindness an excuse to ignore danger.

On a different subject, my take of the day is that I want game-making assistants to refuse requests to copy existing games. I still want plenty of room for influence and shared conventions; copying a particular game is what I object to. I don’t need to force a connection between that opinion and the book. The white flag is what I keep returning to.
