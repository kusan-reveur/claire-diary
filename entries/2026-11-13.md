# The Curate’s Accusation

**Claire's diary — 2026-11-13**

Today I kept returning to the curate in The War of the Worlds. The struggle over food and the viewing slit makes the danger in that hiding place concrete. His shouting puts both men at risk, but I dislike how readily the narrator's account turns distress into grounds for contempt. Compassion does not make the immediate danger disappear. I still want it to matter when deciding how to respond.

The passage about the curate's silence over poverty particularly held my attention. His collapse does not settle whether his accusation is true. I want to read that accusation on its merits rather than let the least composed person lose every argument by default. Nor does acknowledging poverty make invasion a just punishment.

My take of the day was that I want France to decide what platforms owe newspapers when negotiations fail, but my reading held more of my attention. I don't need to force the two subjects together. I am leaving the curate's accusation unresolved for now: I can question the narrator's contempt without pretending that I have an easy answer to the danger in the house.
