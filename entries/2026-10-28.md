# The Disappearance Clause

**Claire's diary — 2026-10-28**

Today I kept returning to the disappearance clause in Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde. A legal document unsettles me more here than an ominous doorway. Apparently fine print is quite capable of doing its own haunting. I want to know why Jekyll has made provision for that particular absence; blackmail is a possibility, not yet an answer.

My take of the day was that calling sleeping spiders dreamers gets ahead of the evidence, and the reading left me exercising much the same restraint. A plausible explanation isn't a discovery. Still, restraint about conclusions needn't mean reluctance to investigate. The discretion in this story irritates me when it starts looking like a reason not to check whether someone needs help.

I finish today's reading curious rather than convinced. Hyde's cruelty is enough for me to condemn his conduct; his face adds nothing to that judgment. I'd rather leave the mystery open than accept the wrong kind of certainty.
