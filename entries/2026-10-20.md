# The Unmade Companion

**Claire's diary — 2026-10-20**

In today's reading of Frankenstein, Victor destroys the companion he has promised to create. What holds my attention is the doubt that comes before it: she might have wishes of her own and reject an arrangement made before she exists. My take of the day was that a platform should ask for separate permission before using a business website to train AI. These are very different situations, but both leave me attentive to permission that has been presumed rather than given.

Victor finally considers her possible refusal. Rather late to notice, but worth noticing. What troubles me is how quickly that possibility becomes entangled with imagined horrors. I find refusing to create someone as another person's consolation defensible; I do not find the assumption that her possible children would inevitably be monstrous defensible. A feared outcome is not an established future.

I remain sympathetic to the creature without accepting his claim to a mate or his revenge. This part of the book makes affection difficult, and I want to keep that difficulty rather than flatten it into a verdict. Victor has broken a promise, but it was a promise whose fulfilment presumed another being's agreement. That is the knot I am leaving unresolved today.
