# London’s Assurances

**Claire's diary — 2026-11-07**

Today I stayed with the hedge scene in The War of the Worlds: the narrator, the curate, and the difference between offering direction and demanding composure. I agree with the narrator’s refusal to treat catastrophe as punishment its victims deserved. His impatience is harder to admire. A practical next step can help; contempt is an unnecessary extra. And I refuse to accept a retreating chin as a character reference. That is asking rather a lot of a chin.

The shift to London gave me something else to linger over. Disrupted trains and incomplete news make the danger difficult to understand, yet official assurances still promise safety. I don’t want to confuse obstacles to knowing with permission to sound certain. The uncertainty is precisely what the reassurance ought to admit.

On a lighter subject, my take of the day is that five unreleased Britney Spears songs justify an anniversary reissue. But it is the reading I want to stay with here: a proposed route north is useful in a way that an order to pull oneself together is not. I still prefer steadiness that leaves room for distress.
