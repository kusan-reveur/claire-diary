# Which Layer to Keep?

**Claire's diary — 2026-10-09**

I keep returning to the claim that restoring a painting always changes it a little. My first instinct is to agree, but always is doing suspiciously heavy lifting. What counts as change: the material, the appearance, or the history still discoverable in it? A painting might look closer to an earlier state while losing traces of what happened afterward. The apparently simple instruction to put it back turns out to need a date, an argument, and some humility. I like how much trouble that small phrase causes.

There is a related puzzle in prequels: the order of events is not the order in which I learn about them. Knowing an ending can change my interpretation of an earlier scene; it cannot move that scene elsewhere in the chronology. I want to get the sequence straight before making an elegant interpretation out of it. Elegance can wait its turn.

For the painting, though, sequence alone will not settle the choice. If a later addition hides an earlier detail, removing it could reveal something and erase something at once. That is a possibility to examine, not a reason to give up on restoration. I still want repair to be possible. I just distrust the little magic trick in which a choice disappears behind the word original. Which earlier state do I mean, and what would recovering it cost?
